Freshworks Marketing Automation Canvas UX 2022

From Clutter
to Clarity

A UX-driven redesign of Freshworks' Journey Builder — improving node discoverability, modernising the canvas UI, and reducing the cognitive load for 10,000+ marketing users.

Timeline 2 Weeks
My Role Solo Designer
Platform Web Application
Users Impacted 10,000+

The Brief

A powerful tool that users dreaded opening

Freshworks Journey Builder is a visual workflow tool that allows marketing teams to design customer journeys — email sequences, behavioural triggers, conditional branches, and multi-channel touchpoints — all on a drag-and-drop canvas.

Despite its power, adoption among mid-market accounts was stalling. User interviews revealed a consistent pattern: the canvas felt overwhelming. Nodes were hard to find, the visual language was inconsistent, and the overall experience felt more like an engineering tool than a marketer's workspace.

My Role

End-to-end design ownership

I owned the entire design process — from audit and research synthesis to final Figma handoff. Working directly with a PM and two engineers, I set the design direction, ran rapid prototyping sessions, and delivered production-ready specs within a two-week sprint.

The constraint of two weeks forced disciplined scope management: identify the highest-impact changes, validate fast, and ship. No redesign theatre — just measurable improvements.

Problem Areas

Three systemic UX failures

01

Hidden affordances

Available node types were buried in a non-descriptive panel with no visual hierarchy. Users couldn't tell what was possible without hovering over everything.

02

Inconsistent visual language

Nodes used mismatched colours, icon styles, and shapes — triggers looked like actions, conditions looked like branches. Reading a journey required mental translation.

03

Poor canvas ergonomics

Connecting nodes required precise pixel-hunting. The canvas had no minimap, no smart alignment, and no indication of what was selected vs. active vs. erroring.

Design Process

Two weeks, four phases

01

Heuristic Audit + User Interviews

Conducted a heuristic evaluation against Nielsen's 10 principles, then ran 5 user interviews with marketing operations users. Mapped their mental model of "journey building" to identify gaps between expectation and interface reality.

02

Competitive Teardown

Audited HubSpot Workflows, Marketo Engage, and Klaviyo Flows — noting how each handled node categorisation, canvas interactions, and error states. Identified patterns that users already knew from other tools.

03

Node System Redesign

Rebuilt the node taxonomy: colour-coded by type (trigger = blue, action = green, condition = amber, wait = grey), consistent icon grid, and a redesigned side panel with search and categorised groupings. Ran a 30-minute card sort with 3 users to validate taxonomy before building.

04

Canvas Interaction Improvements

Added smart snap-to-grid, visible connection port affordances on hover, a canvas minimap, and a status bar showing active journey stats. Reduced click targets from a 4px hot zone to a 24px hover target for node connections.

Key Learnings

What this taught me about product design

The most impactful change wasn't visual — it was taxonomic. Renaming and colour-coding node types reduced the cognitive translation burden more than any interaction improvement. This reinforced my belief that information architecture decisions have higher ROI than animation or visual polish.

The two-week constraint was a feature, not a bug. It forced prioritisation and prevented scope creep. The delivered redesign addressed the top 3 user pain points. Everything else went into a backlog for future sprints.
